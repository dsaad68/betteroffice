## Reference vs candidate

The slide is a "Partner Questionnaire" content card: a bold navy header bar, a blue side panel labeled "General" with a people icon, and a numbered five-item list on a light gray content panel. An oversized red "Minor cleanup" annotation box hangs off the right edge of the slide in both renders. LibreOffice draws the header and "General" label in bold, tightly-kerned text, numbers each list item ("1." through "5.") with a hanging indent, and renders "Minor cleanup" in solid white. BetterOffice draws the same layout and positions but with regular-weight header/label text, no list numbering or hanging indent (all lines flush left), looser title letter-spacing, and darker, low-contrast "Minor cleanup" text.

## Findings

### 1. Auto-numbered list renders with no bullet numbers or hanging indent

- Where: Rectangle 7 (id 8), the light-gray content panel, region ≈ [0.253, 0.187, 0.979, 0.966].
- Reference: each of the five paragraphs is prefixed with "1." … "5." at the left margin, and text after the number is indented to a common left edge; the wrapped second line of item 5 ("integrate with?") aligns with the indented text, not the numbers.
- Candidate: no numbers are drawn at all; every line (including the wrapped continuation) starts flush at the same left position, with no hanging indent applied.
- XML:
  ```xml
  <a:p><a:pPr marL="514350" indent="-514350" fontAlgn="ctr">
    <a:buFont typeface="+mj-lt"/><a:buAutoNum type="arabicPeriod"/>
  </a:pPr><a:r><a:rPr lang="en-US" sz="2000"/><a:t>Partner Name</a:t></a:r></a:p>
  ```
  (repeated per paragraph, `type="arabicPeriod"` for all five items).
- Why the candidate is wrong: `buAutoNum type="arabicPeriod"` mandates rendering "1." … "5." markers, and `marL="514350" indent="-514350"` is a standard hanging-indent pair (first line at `marL+indent`=0 relative to the bullet, wrapped lines at `marL`). Neither the bullet glyphs nor the hanging indent show up in the candidate.

### 2. Bold run weight not applied to title and panel label

- Where: Rectangle 2 (id 3), "Partner Questionnaire" header, region ≈ [0.022, 0.053, 0.979, 0.139]; Rectangle 9 (id 10), "General" panel label inside the blue sidebar.
- Reference: both strings render in a heavy/bold weight consistent with the deck's other headers.
- Candidate: both strings render in a thin regular weight, clearly lighter than the reference.
- XML: `<a:rPr lang="en-US" sz="3600" b="1" spc="-100">...<a:t>Partner Questionnaire </a:t>` and `<a:rPr lang="en-US" sz="2800" b="1">...<a:t>General</a:t>`.
- Why the candidate is wrong: `b="1"` explicitly requests bold on both runs; the candidate ignores it and falls back to the base (non-bold) weight of the resolved font.

### 3. Title character spacing (kerning) not applied

- Where: Rectangle 2 (id 3), "Partner Questionnaire" header, region ≈ [0.022, 0.053, 0.979, 0.139].
- Reference: letters are tightly packed, matching the reduced tracking.
- Candidate: letters are spaced at normal/default tracking, making the string visibly wider (contributing to the header text spanning further right than in the reference).
- XML: `<a:rPr lang="en-US" sz="3600" b="1" spc="-100" ...>`.
- Why the candidate is wrong: `spc="-100"` (hundredths of a point) requests -1pt of tracking between characters; the candidate's letter spacing matches untracked default text instead.

### 4. Degenerate gradient fill on "Minor cleanup" run not resolved to white

- Where: Rectangle 10 (id 11), the red annotation box hanging off the right slide edge, region ≈ [0.871, 0.083, 1.131, 0.889].
- Reference: "Minor cleanup" renders in solid white, legible against the red fill.
- Candidate: the text renders in a darker, low-contrast color (reads as a default/fallback text color), hard to read against the red fill.
- XML: `<a:rPr lang="en-US" sz="2400"><a:gradFill><a:gsLst><a:gs pos="0"><a:srgbClr val="FFFFFF"/></a:gs><a:gs pos="100000"><a:srgbClr val="FFFFFF"/></a:gs></a:gsLst><a:lin ang="5400000" scaled="0"/></a:gradFill>...</a:rPr>`.
- Why the candidate is wrong: both gradient stops are `FFFFFF`, i.e. a degenerate (solid-white) gradient. Any correct evaluation of the gradient at any position yields white, matching the reference; the candidate instead appears to skip run-level `gradFill` entirely and fall back to a default text color.
