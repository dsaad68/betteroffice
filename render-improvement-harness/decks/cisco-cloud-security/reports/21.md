## Reference vs candidate

Slide 21 is a "Next Steps" list: a title, then three horizontal banner rows, each a colored numbered badge ("1", "2", "3") followed by a light-gray bar with a description sentence. LibreOffice renders all six banners as plain horizontal bars with upright, left-to-right text. BetterOffice draws the same six bars in the same positions and colors, but every piece of text inside them — the three digits and the three sentences — comes out rotated 90° from upright, laid out as loosely spaced vertical/sideways glyph runs instead of horizontal sentences. The footer slide-number also prints its unresolved field code rather than "21".

## Findings

### 1. Text inside rotated banner shapes is not counter-rotated to upright

- Where: all six banner shapes — digit badges "Round Same Side Corner Rectangle 5/10/12" (ids 6, 11, 13) and label bars "Round Same Side Corner Rectangle 4/11/13" (ids 5, 12, 14); combined region ~[0.046, 0.234, 0.619, 1.332].
- Reference: each shape is a plain axis-aligned bar (built from a tall/narrow rectangle rotated 90°/270° about its center so the final outline lands horizontal) with fully horizontal, upright text — "1", "2", "3" in the badges and full sentences in the label bars.
- Candidate: the bar outlines land in the correct horizontal position and color (rotation of the shape geometry itself is correct), but the text runs inside are rotated 90° from upright: digit "1" reads as a sideways "⊢", digit "2" and "3" are rotated on their sides, and each sentence is drawn as a vertical column of individually-rotated words with large gaps between them, unreadable as running text.
- XML: badge shapes carry `<a:xfrm rot="16200000">` (270°) paired with `<a:bodyPr vert="vert" rtlCol="0" anchor="ctr"/>`; label-bar shapes carry `<a:xfrm rot="5400000">` (90°) paired with `<a:bodyPr vert="vert270" lIns="91440" tIns="45720" rIns="91440" bIns="91440" .../>`. In both cases `bodyPr/@vert` is deliberately the inverse of the shape's own rotation — a standard OOXML pattern for keeping text upright inside a shape whose geometry has been rotated into place.
- Why the candidate is wrong: per ECMA-376 §21.1.2.1.1, `a:bodyPr/@vert` sets the text flow direction within the shape's local (unrotated) frame, applied before the shape's `a:xfrm/@rot` is applied to the whole shape+text as a unit; here the two rotations are chosen specifically to cancel out to horizontal, upright text. The candidate is applying the shape's own rotation but not compensating with the `vert`/`vert270` text direction (or is applying it in the wrong order relative to the shape rotation), leaving text visibly rotated 90° from the reference. All six shapes share this one cause — hot cells r2c2/r2c3/r3c2/r3c3 (the two visible label-bar sentences) are exactly where the sideways text sits.

### 2. Slide-number field prints its placeholder code instead of the resolved number

- Where: footer, bottom-right corner, region ~[0.953, 0.931, 0.971, 0.944].
- Reference: prints "21" (the slide's 1-based index).
- Candidate: prints the literal field-code glyph "‹#›".
- XML: `master.xml`: `<a:fld id="{4ABDCABE-3F10-B64C-92F1-862014417034}" type="slidenum"><a:rPr sz="600" .../><a:t>‹#›</a:t></a:fld>` — same unresolved-field pattern seen on other slides of this deck (e.g. slide 2).
- Why the candidate is wrong: `type="slidenum"` fields must be rendered as the evaluated slide number rather than the cached placeholder text; this is a field-evaluation gap, not an XML content difference.
