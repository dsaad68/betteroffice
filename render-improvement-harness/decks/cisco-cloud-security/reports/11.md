## Reference vs candidate

Slide 11 shows two side-by-side "Securelets" / "Elastica Gateway" process diagrams, each a gray panel with hand-drawn pictogram icons (person at a desk, a 3-person meeting, a cloud, a person with a laptop) connected by dashed arrows, plus a row of partner app logos underneath. The reference renders the title on two full lines, white bold headings on the two blue bars, fully-detailed icons, and colored/recolored app logos. The candidate shows the title's second line mostly erased by the panel behind it, near-invisible gray-on-blue bar headings, several icons reduced to a flat rectangle or missing outright, two logos (OneDrive, the Dropbox mark inside the cloud) missing entirely, the Google Drive logo shown in its original colors instead of duotoned, and the wrong slide-number glyph in the footer. An unrelated red "replace ... crumb boxes ... slide 23" callout box on the left is identical in both and outside the scope of this report.

## Findings

### 1. custGeom pictogram icons render as a plain box or not at all

- Where: both diagram panels, e.g. the person-at-desk icon (Freeform 129, id 130) at `[0.242, 0.39, 0.315, 0.519]`, the 3-person meeting icon (nested freeforms under Group 133, id 134-147) at `[0.242, 0.54, 0.315, 0.67]`, the cloud shape (Freeform 212, id 213) at `[0.437, 0.369, 0.559, 0.489]`, and the person-with-laptop icon (Group 213, id 214-219) at `[0.428, 0.593, 0.492, 0.701]`. Each is duplicated on the Elastica Gateway side (ids 244-267) with the same result.
- Reference: draws the full traced outline — a white silhouette of a person sitting at a monitor, three people at a table, a blue cloud outline, a person with an open laptop.
- Candidate: the person-at-desk icon draws as a plain white rectangle sized to the freeform's bounding box (336118 x 280717 EMU) instead of the path; the cloud draws as a plain blue rectangle instead of a cloud outline; the meeting icon and the laptop icon draw nothing at all, leaving only their background circle.
- XML:
  ```xml
  <a:custGeom>
    <a:gdLst>
      <a:gd name="connsiteX0" fmla="*/ 671761 w 1919765"/>
      <a:gd name="connsiteY0" fmla="*/ 793243 h 1603335"/>
      ...
    </a:gdLst>
    <a:pathLst>
      <a:path w="1919765" h="1603335">
        <a:moveTo>...</a:moveTo>
        <a:cubicBezTo>...</a:cubicBezTo>
        ...
      </a:path>
    </a:pathLst>
  </a:custGeom>
  <a:solidFill><a:schemeClr val="bg1"/></a:solidFill>
  ```
- Why the candidate is wrong: these are ordinary `custGeom` shapes with `connsiteX`/`connsiteY` guide formulas (PowerPoint's "Edit Points" export) and a `<a:path w="1919765" h="1603335">` whose coordinate space is scaled to the shape's much smaller `ext`. The path data is well-formed (moveTo/lnTo/cubicBezTo, closed subpaths) and LibreOffice traces it correctly; the candidate's inconsistent fallback (bounding-box fill for some shapes, nothing for others) points at a bug in custGeom path construction or the guide-formula/scale step, not a missing feature. This is the largest contributor to the slide's diff and explains hot cells r1c1, r1c2, r2c4 and r3c1.

### 2. Header bar text renders in dark gray instead of white

- Where: "Securelets" bar (id 97) and "Elastica Gateway" bar (id 168), region `[0.218, 0.24, 0.969, 0.329]`.
- Reference: bold white text ("Securelets", "Elastica Gateway") on the dark-blue (`tx2`) bar fill.
- Candidate: text renders as `rgb(103,103,103)` (~`#676767`), the theme's generic dark-gray text color, giving very low contrast against the blue fill. Measured directly from the render: reference pixels under the glyphs are near-white/light-blue anti-aliasing, candidate pixels are `(103,103,103)`/`(96,103,112)`.
- XML:
  ```xml
  <p:style>
    <a:fillRef idx="1"><a:schemeClr val="accent1"/></a:fillRef>
    <a:fontRef idx="minor"><a:schemeClr val="lt1"/></a:fontRef>
  </p:style>
  <p:txBody>...<a:rPr lang="en-US" sz="1600" b="1" dirty="0"/><a:t>Securelets</a:t>...</p:txBody>
  ```
- Why the candidate is wrong: the run has no explicit `<a:solidFill>`, so its color must come from the shape's style matrix `fontRef`, which points at `schemeClr val="lt1"` (theme "light 1", white). The candidate instead falls back to the generic `676767` default text color — the same value used for body/title text elsewhere on the deck — indicating the `p:style/a:fontRef` scheme color isn't being resolved for text without an explicit run color.

### 3. Picture blipFill color effects mishandled (biLevel drops the image, duotone is ignored)

- Where: OneDrive logo (Picture 78, id 197) at `[0.331, 0.754, 0.395, 0.863]`, the Dropbox mark inside the cloud icon (`pasted-image.pdf`, id 230) at `[0.47, 0.404, 0.509, 0.469]`, and the Google Drive logo (Picture 6, id 194) at `[0.252, 0.754, 0.314, 0.864]`.
- Reference: OneDrive and Dropbox logos show as white marks (biLevel thresholded); Google Drive shows as a white/gray duotoned mark on its navy circle.
- Candidate: OneDrive's circle is left completely blank (no logo at all) and the Dropbox mark inside the cloud is likewise absent; Google Drive shows its original multi-color (green/yellow/blue) artwork, i.e. the duotone recolor was skipped.
- XML:
  ```xml
  <!-- OneDrive, id 197 -->
  <a:blip r:embed="rId10"><a:biLevel thresh="25000"/></a:blip>
  <!-- Dropbox mark, id 230 -->
  <a:blip r:embed="rId8"><a:biLevel thresh="25000"/></a:blip>
  <!-- Google Drive, id 194 -->
  <a:blip r:embed="rId11">
    <a:duotone><a:schemeClr val="bg2"><a:shade val="45000"/><a:satMod val="135000"/></a:schemeClr><a:prstClr val="white"/></a:duotone>
  </a:blip>
  ```
- Why the candidate is wrong: all three `r:embed` targets (`image44.png`, `image45.png`/`rId10`, `image14.png`/`rId11`) are plain PNGs present in the package — not an unsupported container format — so the failure is in the blipFill color-effect handling, not the decode step. `a:biLevel` should threshold the image to two tones (it should still be visible, just monochrome); instead the picture is dropped entirely. `a:duotone` should recolor the image toward the bg2/white pair; instead it's ignored and the source colors pass through unchanged.

### 4. Title's second line pushed down into the panel behind it

- Where: Title 1 (id 2), region `[0.048, 0.066, 0.961, 0.209]`.
- Reference: both lines ("Redirect your traffic through Cisco Cloud" / "Access Security") are fully visible, tightly spaced, sitting entirely above the light-gray diagram panel (Rectangle 94/95) that starts around slide-fraction y=0.232.
- Candidate: measuring the rendered glyphs, line 1 starts ~14px lower than the reference (row 48 vs row 34 in the 540px-tall render) and line 2 is only visible for its top ~16px (rows 97-112) before the panel behind it, which is later in paint order and opaque, cuts it off.
- XML:
  ```xml
  <!-- master titleStyle lvl1pPr -->
  <a:lnSpc><a:spcPct val="80000"/></a:lnSpc>
  <!-- master title placeholder bodyPr -->
  <a:bodyPr ... anchor="ctr" .../>
  <!-- slide11.xml title -->
  <a:bodyPr/> <!-- no override -->
  ```
- Why the candidate is wrong: nothing in the slide/layout/master overrides the title's `anchor="ctr"` or `80%` line spacing, so the two engines are working from the same numbers, yet the candidate's two-line block sits measurably lower and taller than the reference's. Whatever the exact vertical-metrics difference (line-height computation for `spcPct` line spacing, or how the centered block's total height is measured), the practical effect is that the second title line is occluded by a shape drawn after it, which a reader would notice immediately.

### 5. Slide number field prints its literal fallback text

- Where: master slide-number field, region `[0.927, 0.917, 1.0, 0.954]` (bottom-right footer).
- Reference: "11".
- Candidate: the field's cached placeholder text `‹#›` is drawn verbatim (renders as an unrelated symbol/tofu glyph in the deck's footer font) instead of being evaluated to the slide's number.
- XML: `<a:fld id="{4ABDCABE-3F10-B64C-92F1-862014417034}" type="slidenum">...<a:t>‹#›</a:t></a:fld>` (master.xml).
- Why the candidate is wrong: per the OOXML field model, `<a:t>` inside an `<a:fld>` is only a cached fallback for consumers that don't recompute fields; a rendering engine is expected to substitute the live value (current slide index) for `type="slidenum"`. LibreOffice does this; the candidate is printing the cached string as literal text.
