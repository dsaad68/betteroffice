## Reference vs candidate

This slide illustrates a 5-step "identify cloud apps" workflow: a two-line gray title, a green "Audit" card and a translucent "Panel C" card on the left (each with a small circular icon), a laptop mockup showing the Audit dashboard in the center, and a numbered list of five green rounded-rectangle steps on the right. The reference renders both left-hand cards as solid, correctly-sized colored panels with legible white captions, the laptop screen cleanly cropped with no OS chrome below it, and the title fully clear of the content below. The candidate is missing the fill on both left-hand cards entirely, replaces their small icon pictures with an uncropped thumbnail of the source artwork (complete with a stray "Audit" caption baked into the image), renders the "Audit" caption text mirrored and in the wrong color (and drops the "Panel C" caption out of view behind the laptop bezel), shows a strip of macOS Dock icons under the laptop screen that should have been cropped off, and clips the second line of the title under the content panel that starts underneath it.

## Findings

### 1. "Audit" and "Panel C" card fills are missing

- Where: `Rectangle 230` (id 231, inside `Group 229`/id 230) and `Rectangle 233` (id 234, inside `Group 232`/id 233). Region `[0.021, 0.353, 0.31, 0.775]` (both cards together, left third of the slide).
- Reference: both cards are solid green (`99CC31`) rounded to their full ~133x228px and ~138x228px boxes.
- Candidate: both boxes are entirely blank/white — no green fill anywhere in that region except for the small stray picture described in finding 2.
- XML:
  ```
  <p:grpSp><p:cNvPr id="230" name="Group 229"/>
    <p:grpSpPr><a:solidFill><a:srgbClr val="99CC31"/></a:solidFill></p:grpSpPr>
    <p:sp><p:cNvPr id="231" name="Rectangle 230"/>
      <p:spPr><a:xfrm rot="10800000" flipV="1">...</a:xfrm>
        <a:prstGeom prst="rect"/><a:grpFill/><a:ln><a:noFill/></a:ln></p:spPr>
      ...
  ```
- Why the candidate is wrong: `<a:grpFill/>` means "use the fill of the immediately enclosing `<p:grpSp>`", which explicitly declares `solidFill srgbClr 99CC31` in its `grpSpPr`. Both group shapes (229 and 232) carry this same declaration, and both of their child rectangles use `<a:grpFill/>`. The candidate paints neither, so group-fill inheritance for shapes nested inside a `p:grpSp` appears unimplemented or not reached here.

### 2. Card icon pictures render the full uncropped source image instead of the `srcRect` crop

- Where: `Picture 231` (id 232) and `Picture 234` (id 235), both `r:embed="rId5"` → `ppt/media/image36.png` (a 195x236px card thumbnail reading "Audit / Panel B caption" on a green background with a white circular icon in the lower right). Region `[0.091, 0.643, 0.304, 0.763]`.
- Reference: shows only the small white circular icon (arrows/dots), cropped tightly per `srcRect`, at each picture's small ~60x65px box.
- Candidate: shows the entire source thumbnail — including the green background and the "Audit" caption text — squashed into the same small box, which is what reads as a shrunken green square with tiny mirrored-looking text sitting where the Audit card should be.
- XML:
  ```
  <p:blipFill rotWithShape="1">
    <a:blip r:embed="rId5" cstate="print">...</a:blip>
    <a:srcRect l="17102" t="25801"/>
    <a:stretch/>
  </p:blipFill>
  ```
- Why the candidate is wrong: `<a:srcRect l="17102" t="25801"/>` instructs the renderer to discard the left 17.1% and top 25.8% of the source bitmap before stretching the remainder to fill the shape; both instances use the exact same crop and same source image but only differ in `off`. The candidate's output pixel-matches the *uncropped* source scaled to the box, i.e. `srcRect` is not being applied to this picture fill.

### 3. "Audit" caption text is mirrored and mis-colored, though correctly positioned

- Where: the text run "Audit" inside `Rectangle 230` (id 231). Measured bbox in both renders: x 60-112, y 298-311 in the reference; x 60-112, y 296-311 in the candidate (i.e. same position/size).
- Reference: reads "Audit", upright, bold, solid white (`lt1`), roughly 16pt, centered in the green card.
- Candidate: reads "tibuA" — a horizontally-mirrored rendering of the glyphs (not merely reversed character order: the 'd' in "Audit" becomes a mirrored shape that looks like 'b') — and is colored solid gray, RGB (103,103,103) / `676767`, not white.
- XML:
  ```
  <p:sp><p:cNvPr id="231" name="Rectangle 230"/>
    <p:spPr><a:xfrm rot="10800000" flipV="1"><a:off x="1980832" y="717630"/><a:ext cx="1261872" cy="2170239"/></a:xfrm>...</p:spPr>
    <p:style><a:fontRef idx="minor"><a:schemeClr val="lt1"/></a:fontRef></p:style>
    <p:txBody><a:bodyPr wrap="square" anchor="ctr"><a:noAutofit/></a:bodyPr>
      <a:p><a:pPr algn="ctr"/><a:r><a:rPr lang="en-IN" sz="1600" b="1" dirty="0"/><a:t>Audit</a:t></a:r></a:p>
    </p:txBody>
  </p:sp>
  ```
- Why the candidate is wrong: `rot="10800000"` (180deg) combined with `flipV="1"` is the standard OOXML idiom for a horizontal-only flip of a shape's fill/outline; PowerPoint and the reference renderer keep the shape's *text* glyphs upright regardless of this flip (only the geometry/fill mirrors), which is why the reference still reads "Audit" normally. The candidate appears to apply the flip to the rasterized text itself. Separately, the run carries no own `<a:solidFill>`, so per the OOXML style-matrix its color must resolve through `p:style/a:fontRef` (`schemeClr lt1`, theme white) — exactly as the reference does — while the candidate falls back to the generic default text gray `676767`.

### 4. Title's second line is pushed down and clipped by the panel drawn beneath it

- Where: `Title 10` (id 11), text "Identify which cloud apps are being used in your environment". Box inherited from the layout: `off (310855,341313)` `ext (8469608,731837)` → frac `[0.034, 0.066, 0.960, 0.209]`.
- Reference: line 1 spans render rows ~35-74, line 2 spans ~76-115 (measured on dark-pixel rows), both fully above row ~125 where `Group 237`'s gray `Rectangle 43` panel begins.
- Candidate: line 1 starts ~13px lower (row ~48) and line 2 starts ~14px lower too (row ~90+), so line 2 runs into the panel's top edge at row ~125; since `Rectangle 43` (id 44) is drawn after the title in document order, it paints over the lower half of "your environment," leaving only the tops of its letters visible.
- XML:
  ```
  <!-- master.xml, titleStyle/lvl1pPr -->
  <a:lnSpc><a:spcPct val="80000"/></a:lnSpc>
  ```
  ```
  <!-- slide8.xml, Title 10 -- no bodyPr/lstStyle overrides -->
  <p:sp><p:nvPr><p:ph type="title"/></p:nvPr>
  <p:spPr/><p:txBody><a:bodyPr/><a:lstStyle/>
    <a:p><a:r><a:rPr lang="en-US"/><a:t>Identify which cloud apps are being used in your environment</a:t></a:r></a:p>
  </p:txBody></p:sp>
  ```
- Why the candidate is wrong: nothing on the slide, layout, or master repositions or resizes this placeholder differently between the two renders, and the master's 80% line spacing is the only spacing rule in play; the reference already overflows the ~77px-tall box by design (no autofit anywhere) but keeps both lines clear of row 125. The candidate's uniform ~13-14px downward offset of both lines indicates the title block's vertical start (not its line spacing, which is consistent at ~42px between lines in both renders) is computed differently, and this is the same title-clipping pattern already isolated on slide 6 of this deck.

### 5. Laptop screen mockup shows macOS Dock icons that should be cropped off

- Where: `Picture 8` (id 27, the browser/dashboard screenshot overlaid on the laptop bezel). Region `[0.252, 0.37, 0.603, 0.759]`.
- Reference: the screen content ends cleanly at the bottom of the browser chrome; no dock or desktop is visible.
- Candidate: a row of colorful macOS Dock application icons is visible along the bottom edge of the screen, cut off mid-row by the shape's own bottom edge.
- XML:
  ```
  <p:blipFill rotWithShape="1">
    <a:blip r:embed="rId9"/>
    <a:srcRect t="251" b="16720"/>
    <a:stretch/>
  </p:blipFill>
  ```
- Why the candidate is wrong: `b="16720"` instructs the renderer to discard the bottom 16.72% of the source screenshot (where the Dock lives) before stretching into the shape; the candidate's extra visible content below the browser chrome is consistent with this bottom crop not being applied, the same underlying `srcRect`-handling gap as finding 2.
