## Reference vs candidate

Slide 17 is "Conduct forensic analysis on historical data": a MacBook mockup showing two overlapping product-screenshot windows on the left, and a stack of four blue rounded-rectangle callouts (numbered 1-4) with white text on the right. LibreOffice renders the laptop with a crisp black bezel and clean screenshot content, and four solid cyan-blue callout bars with legible white numbers and body text. BetterOffice renders the callout bars with no fill at all (leaving their white text nearly invisible on the pale backdrop), shows the digits in dark gray instead of white, and renders the laptop mockup with a partially transparent bezel plus a stray OS X dock strip bleeding into the screen area.

## Findings

### 1. Callout bars lose their `grpFill`-inherited blue background

- Where: Rounded Rectangle 23/20/17/14 (ids 60, 63, 66, 69), one per numbered item, region [0.635, 0.307, 0.952, 0.815].
- Reference: each bar is filled solid cyan-blue (`13A7E0`), with white body text and a thin white vertical divider clearly legible on top.
- Candidate: the bars render with no fill (transparent, showing the light-gray card background through), so the white text and white divider — both of which *do* carry their own explicit white color and render correctly — become nearly invisible against the near-white backdrop.
- XML:
```xml
<!-- Group 10, id 7 -->
<p:grpSpPr><a:xfrm>...</a:xfrm><a:solidFill><a:srgbClr val="13A7E0"/></a:solidFill></p:grpSpPr>
<!-- Rounded Rectangle 23, id 60 -->
<p:spPr><a:xfrm>...</a:xfrm><a:prstGeom prst="roundRect"><a:avLst/></a:prstGeom><a:grpFill/><a:ln><a:noFill/></a:ln><a:effectLst/></p:spPr>
```
- Why the candidate is wrong: `<a:grpFill/>` means "use whatever fill the parent group declares", here the group's own `13A7E0` solid fill. All four groups (ids 7, 6, 5, 3) repeat the identical pattern. BetterOffice does not resolve `grpFill`, leaving the shape unfilled instead of blue — this is the same bug already documented on this deck's slide 07 (icon glyphs) and slide 09 (a missing green box), and it is the single largest contributor to this slide's diff (hot cells r3c4 70.8%, r2c4 55.9%, both over the callout column).

### 2. List-item digits render in the wrong (dark) color

- Where: Rectangle 25/22/19/16 (ids 62, 65, 68, 71), the digits "1"-"4", region [0.635, 0.326, 0.677, 0.796].
- Reference: each digit is bold white, matching the callout's body text.
- Candidate: each digit renders in a dark gray/black, close to the theme's generic default text color.
- XML:
```xml
<p:style>
  <a:fillRef idx="1"><a:schemeClr val="accent1"/></a:fillRef>
  <a:fontRef idx="minor"><a:schemeClr val="lt1"/></a:fontRef>
</p:style>
<p:txBody><a:p><a:pPr algn="ctr"/><a:r><a:rPr lang="en-US" sz="1200" b="1" dirty="0"/><a:t>1</a:t></a:r></a:p></p:txBody>
```
- Why the candidate is wrong: the run has no `<a:solidFill>` of its own, so its color must resolve through the shape's style-matrix `fontRef` (`schemeClr lt1`, theme white), exactly as the reference does. Once finding 1 is fixed the fill will be blue again, so a dark digit would still be nearly unreadable against it — this is a separate, additional bug from the missing box fill.

### 3. Laptop mockup and screenshot pictures ignore their `srcRect` crops

- Where: Picture 2 (id 83, the MacBook frame image) and Picture 8 (id 85, the background browser screenshot), region [0.185, 0.276, 0.643, 0.8].
- Reference: a tight black bezel frames the screen on all four sides, and the visible screen area shows only the two overlapping browser windows against a plain blue desktop background.
- Candidate: sampling the rendered pixels confirms the bug directly — at (230,300) and (560,300), just inside the bezel's left/right edge, the reference is solid black `(0,0,0)` but the candidate is the slide's gray background color `(242,242,242)`; at the bottom edge (235,392)/(555,392) the reference is `(0,0,0)` but the candidate is a washed `(74,75,77)`. Re-rendering `image37.png` stretched into the same box *without* applying its `srcRect` crop reproduces these exact candidate colors (`(255,255,255)` and `(66,67,68)` respectively), while applying the documented crop reproduces the reference's `(0,0,0)`. Visually, this also lets an OS X dock strip (present in the source `image38.png` below its intended crop line) bleed into the bottom of the laptop screen.
- XML: `Picture 2: <a:blip r:embed="rId13"/><a:srcRect l="6360" r="6360" b="8637"/><a:stretch/>`; `Picture 8: <a:blip r:embed="rId14"/><a:srcRect t="251" b="16720"/><a:stretch/>`
- Why the candidate is wrong: `a:srcRect` values are 1000ths-of-a-percent crop insets that must be applied to the source bitmap before stretching into the shape's frame. BetterOffice stretches the full, uncropped bitmap instead, which (a) shows the source's transparent/white margin around the laptop artwork where a solid black bezel should be, and (b) reveals off-crop content (the dock) at the bottom of the screenshot. This exact `srcRect`-ignored pattern is already documented on this deck's slides 08, 12, and 15.

### 4. Slide-number field shows literal field markup instead of the computed number

- Where: the master's footer slide-number placeholder, bottom-right corner, region [0.94, 0.955, 0.98, 0.98].
- Reference: renders "17".
- Candidate: renders "‹#›" verbatim.
- XML: `master.xml: <a:fld id="{4ABDCABE-3F10-B64C-92F1-862014417034}" type="slidenum">...<a:t>‹#›</a:t></a:fld>`
- Why the candidate is wrong: `<a:t>‹#›</a:t>` inside an `<a:fld type="slidenum">` is only the cached fallback text; a renderer with field support is expected to substitute the shape's actual position in the deck, as the reference does. Small, low-contrast (25% alpha, 6pt) element, so it barely affects readability, but it's a concrete, reproducible mismatch, and the same issue already appears on nearly every other slide in this deck.
