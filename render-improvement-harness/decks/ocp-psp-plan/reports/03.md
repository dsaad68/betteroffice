## Reference vs candidate

Slide 3 is a circular "engine" diagram: a photo of rocks in water (Picture 8) clipped into a crescent, framed by a navy ring, fading into the white content area on the right, with curved WordArt labels ("Powering Partner GROWTH", "Deepening COMMUNITY Connection") following the ring. To the right, four columns of plain text rows list engines, key activities, and a fiscal year big rocks. The reference (LibreOffice) shows the full photographic collage with the curved ring, curved WordArt text, and a soft gradient fade into the white background. The candidate (BetterOffice) instead shows a solid black rectangle covering the same left ~29% of the slide, with only a thin sliver of navy visible at its right edge and no photo, no ring curve, and no arc-warped text (the two WordArt labels appear as straight diagonal lines cutting across the black area). The right-hand text columns match closely between the two renders.

## Findings

### 1. custGeom crescent/circle shapes render as their bounding rectangle

- Where: Freeform: Shape 11 (id 12, region [0.0, 0.0, 0.291, 1.0]), Freeform: Shape 14 (id 15, [0.009, 0.0, 0.3, 1.0]), Picture 8 (id 9, [-0.0, 0.0, 0.291, 1.0]), Freeform: Shape 59 (id 60, [0.008, 0.0, 0.43, 1.0]), Freeform: Shape 74 (id 75, [0.109, 0.001, 0.531, 1.001])
- Reference: each shape is clipped to a custom path — two cubic Bezier arcs meeting at a point — producing a lens/crescent outline. Stacked together (photo, navy ring, two translucent fade layers) they form the circular collage with a soft curved edge fading into the page.
- Candidate: all five shapes paint their full axis-aligned bounding box (a plain rectangle) instead of the Bezier-defined outline. Stacked in z-order, the opaque ones (see finding 2) blot out everything beneath them, leaving a flat-edged rectangle where a curved collage should be.
- XML (id 12's path, representative of all five — 9, 12, 15, 60, 75 share the same construction):
```xml
<a:custGeom>
  <a:pathLst>
    <a:path w="3546928" h="6858000">
      <a:moveTo><a:pt x="117928" y="0"/></a:moveTo>
      <a:cubicBezTo><a:pt x="2011712" y="0"/><a:pt x="3546928" y="1535216"/><a:pt x="3546928" y="3429000"/></a:cubicBezTo>
      <a:cubicBezTo><a:pt x="3546928" y="5322784"/><a:pt x="2011712" y="6858000"/><a:pt x="117928" y="6858000"/></a:cubicBezTo>
      <a:lnTo><a:pt x="0" y="6852045"/></a:lnTo>
      <a:lnTo><a:pt x="0" y="5955"/></a:lnTo>
      <a:close/>
    </a:path>
  </a:pathLst>
</a:custGeom>
```
- Why the candidate is wrong: the path explicitly curves via `cubicBezTo`; the candidate fills/clips to the shape's `a:ext` box instead of tracing this path, discarding the curve for every shape that uses it on this slide (including the picture's clip).

### 2. Alpha modifier on schemeClr solidFill is dropped

- Where: Freeform: Shape 11 (id 12, [0.0, 0.0, 0.291, 1.0]), Freeform: Shape 59 (id 60, [0.008, 0.0, 0.43, 1.0]), Freeform: Shape 74 (id 75, [0.109, 0.001, 0.531, 1.001])
- Reference: id 12 is a 60%-alpha dark (`tx2`) shadow layer; ids 60 and 75 are 50%-alpha, 95%-`lumMod` near-white (`bg1`) layers used to fade the photo into the page. All three are meant to be see-through.
- Candidate: all three render fully opaque. Combined with finding 1 (bounding-box fill instead of curved clip), the opaque id 12 rectangle sits on top of the picture and the navy ring and covers nearly the entire left panel in solid near-black, which is why the panel reads as a flat black block rather than a faded photo collage.
- XML:
```xml
<!-- id 12 -->
<a:solidFill><a:schemeClr val="tx2"><a:alpha val="60000"/></a:schemeClr></a:solidFill>
<!-- id 60 / id 75 -->
<a:solidFill><a:schemeClr val="bg1"><a:lumMod val="95000"/><a:alpha val="50000"/></a:schemeClr></a:solidFill>
```
- Why the candidate is wrong: `a:alpha` is a direct, unambiguous transparency modifier on the resolved scheme color; dropping it changes these layers from translucent overlays into opaque fills, which is visibly the dominant defect on this slide.

### 3. WordArt arc warp not applied to curved ring labels

- Where: Rectangle 52 (id 53, "Powering Partner GROWTH", [0.101, 0.253, 0.376, 0.49], rot 4358177/60000 ≈ 72.6°), Rectangle 54 (id 55, "Deepening COMMUNITY Connection", [0.064, 0.593, 0.377, 0.837], rot 18006903/60000 ≈ 300.1°)
- Reference: text is warped along an arc (`textArchUp` for id 53, `textArchDown` for id 55), so each letter follows the ring's curvature.
- Candidate: text is drawn as a straight line and only the shape's rectangular rotation is applied, so the label cuts diagonally across the panel instead of curving with the ring.
- XML:
```xml
<a:bodyPr ...>
  <a:prstTxWarp prst="textArchUp"><a:avLst/></a:prstTxWarp>
  <a:spAutoFit/>
</a:bodyPr>
```
(id 55 has `prst="textArchDown"`)
- Why the candidate is wrong: `a:prstTxWarp` is a distinct preset text-warp geometry from the shape's own rotation; the shape rotation should be applied on top of the warped glyph outlines, not substituted for them.

### 4. LibreOffice wraps `wrap="none"` labels mid-word (lo-suspect)

- Where: Rectangle 40 (id 41, "ENGINE", [0.258, 0.049, 0.32, 0.085]), Rectangle 50 (id 51, "KEY ACTIVITIES", [0.368, 0.029, 0.455, 0.101])
- Reference: "ENGINE" is split into "ENGIN" / "E" on two lines; "KEY ACTIVITIES" is split into "KEY" / "ACTIVITI" / "ES" on three lines, breaking mid-word.
- Candidate: both labels render on a single unwrapped line, overflowing the shape's small box.
- XML:
```xml
<a:bodyPr rot="0" ... wrap="none" ...><a:prstTxWarp prst="textNoShape"/><a:spAutoFit/></a:bodyPr>
```
- Why the candidate is wrong (it isn't): `wrap="none"` combined with `spAutoFit` means the shape should grow to fit one unbroken line, not wrap — let alone break inside a word. BetterOffice's single-line, unwrapped output matches the XML; LibreOffice's mid-word wrapping is the reference behaving incorrectly here.
