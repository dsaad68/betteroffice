## Reference vs candidate

Slide 4 holds a single stacked horizontal bar chart ("Industry Trends") with four categories and three series (Series 1 yellow, Series 2 dark gray, Series 3 light gray). LibreOffice renders it with Category 4 at the top down to Category 1 at the bottom, a horizontal value axis at the bottom (0-14 by 2s), full category names on the left, a centered letter-spaced title, and a bottom-center legend. BetterOffice draws the same bars and colors but reverses the category order, misplaces the value-axis tick labels into the category-label column (truncating the category names to "Categ"), leaves the bottom of the chart unlabeled, auto-scales the value axis to raw data (0-12.3 by 3.1s), puts the legend top-right, left-aligns the un-tracked title, and adds unwanted value labels at the end of every bar.

## Findings

### 1. Category axis order is reversed

- Where: `Chart 10` (id 11), catAx `579760832`, whole plot area, region `[0.061, 0.20, 0.90, 0.80]`.
- Reference: top-to-bottom rows are Category 4, Category 3, Category 2, Category 1 (category index 0 at the bottom, index 3 at the top).
- Candidate: top-to-bottom rows are Category 1, Category 2, Category 3, Category 4 (index 0 at the top, index 3 at the bottom) — confirmed by matching each row's segment widths to the series values (top row = 4.3/2.4/2 = Category 1; bottom row = 4.5/2.8/5 = Category 4).
- XML: `<c:catAx><c:axId val="579760832"/><c:scaling><c:orientation val="minMax"/></c:scaling>...<c:axPos val="l"/>`
- Why the candidate is wrong: `orientation val="minMax"` on a left-positioned category axis places the first category at the axis minimum (bottom) and the last at the maximum (top), which is what LibreOffice does. BetterOffice plots the opposite order without any `maxMin` in the XML.

### 2. Value-axis labels misplaced into the category column; category names clipped

- Where: `Chart 10` (id 11), left label column, region `[0.061, 0.138, 0.20, 0.806]`.
- Reference: category names ("Category 1"-"Category 4") are fully shown on the left; numeric value-axis labels (0, 2, 4, ... 14) run horizontally along the bottom.
- Candidate: numeric value labels (0, 3.1, 6.2, 9.2, 12.3) are stacked vertically along the left edge at each row boundary, sharing the column with the category names, which are hard-clipped to "Categ" for all four rows. The bottom of the chart shows a bare axis line with no numbers at all.
- XML: `<c:valAx>...<c:axPos val="b"/>...` and `<c:catAx>...<c:axPos val="l"/>...`
- Why the candidate is wrong: the value axis is explicitly `axPos="b"` (bottom, horizontal) and the category axis `axPos="l"` (left, vertical, single label column). BetterOffice draws the value-axis tick labels in the category axis's column instead of below the plot area, starving the category labels of width.

### 3. Value axis auto-scale does not round to friendly increments

- Where: `Chart 10` (id 11), valAx `579764440`, region `[0.061, 0.138, 0.20, 0.806]`.
- Reference: axis runs 0 to 14 in steps of 2 — comfortably above the largest stack total (12.3).
- Candidate: axis runs 0 to 12.3 in steps of 3.075 (displayed as 3.1), i.e. exactly the largest stacked total divided into 4 equal steps.
- XML: no `c:max`, `c:min`, or `c:majorUnit` is set on `<c:valAx>`, so both engines are auto-computing the scale; `<c:numFmt formatCode="General" sourceLinked="1"/>` means the displayed values are whatever the auto-scale produces.
- Why the candidate is wrong: neither engine's choice is mandated by the file, but tying the axis max/step directly to the raw data total (instead of rounding to a human-friendly max/interval) produces non-round, harder-to-read tick values; LibreOffice's nice-number behavior is the conventional one.

### 4. Legend drawn top-right instead of bottom-center

- Where: `Chart 10` (id 11) legend, reference region `[0.398, 0.778, 0.617, 0.806]`; candidate draws it at roughly `[0.86, 0.18, 0.92, 0.24]`, overlapping the plot area.
- Reference: legend centered below the plot area.
- Candidate: legend in the top-right corner, overlapping the chart title's row and the top of the plot area.
- XML: `<c:legend><c:legendPos val="b"/>`
- Why the candidate is wrong: `legendPos="b"` is explicit; BetterOffice ignores it.

### 5. Chart title left-aligned instead of centered

- Where: `Chart 10` (id 11) title, region `[0.061, 0.12, 0.939, 0.18]`.
- Reference: "INDUSTRY TRENDS" is centered horizontally over the chart frame.
- Candidate: title starts flush against the chart frame's left edge.
- XML: `<c:title><c:tx><c:rich><a:bodyPr ... anchor="ctr" anchorCtr="1"/>` with no explicit `c:layout` (manual position), so the title should use the default auto-layout, which centers it over the plot area.
- Why the candidate is wrong: with no manual layout box given, the title should be auto-positioned centered above the plot area, as LibreOffice does; BetterOffice appears to anchor it to the chart frame's left edge instead.

### 6. Title missing letter-spacing

- Where: `Chart 10` (id 11) title run, region `[0.061, 0.12, 0.35, 0.18]`.
- Reference: "INDUSTRY TRENDS" is rendered with wide letter-spacing (tracked caps).
- Candidate: same text rendered with normal/tight kerning.
- XML: `<a:r><a:rPr lang="en-IN" spc="300" dirty="0"/><a:t>INDUSTRY TRENDS</a:t></a:r>`
- Why the candidate is wrong: `spc="300"` (3pt extra tracking per character) is set on the title run; BetterOffice does not apply it.

### 7. Spurious end-of-bar value labels

- Where: `Chart 10` (id 11), end of every bar (all four rows), region `[0.60, 0.20, 0.88, 0.78]`.
- Reference: no labels are drawn at the ends of the bars.
- Candidate: a small number is drawn past the end of each bar — 2, 2, 3, 5 for Category 1 through 4 respectively, matching the Series 3 (last-stacked) values exactly.
- XML: `<c:dLbls><c:showLegendKey val="0"/><c:showVal val="0"/><c:showCatName val="0"/><c:showSerName val="0"/><c:showPercent val="0"/><c:showBubbleSize val="0"/></c:dLbls>`
- Why the candidate is wrong: every `dLbls` flag, including `showVal`, is `0`; no label of any kind should be drawn, yet BetterOffice shows the Series 3 value at each bar's end.

## Not reported

- Row/gap spacing (bar thickness vs. white space between rows) looks visually tighter in the candidate, but with the category/value labels overlapping it is hard to measure precisely against the `gapWidth val="150"` spec; not filed as a separate finding since it may be a side effect of finding 2.
- Minor anti-aliasing differences on bar edges and axis lines between the two renderers.
- Small sub-pixel differences in exact bar-segment boundary positions, consistent with rounding rather than a data or scale error beyond what's already captured in finding 3.
