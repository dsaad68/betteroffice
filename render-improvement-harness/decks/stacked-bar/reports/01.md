## Reference vs candidate

The slide is a single embedded bar chart ("INDUSTRY TRENDS") showing four categories, each a horizontal stacked bar of three series (Series 1/2/3), with a legend and a numeric value axis. The reference (LibreOffice) draws the category axis fully spelled out on the left ("Category 1"–"Category 4"), the value axis with ticks 0–14 (step 2) along the bottom, a bottom-centered horizontal legend, and no data labels. The candidate (BetterOffice) draws the value axis ticks (0, 3.1, 6.2, 9.2, 12.3) stacked in the same left-hand column as the category labels — truncating every category label to "Categ" — puts the legend as a vertical list top-right, and shows small numeric labels next to the end of each bar that the chart XML explicitly turns off.

## Findings

### 1. Data labels shown despite `showVal`/`showCatName`/etc. all being 0

- Where: Chart 10 (id 11), small digits immediately right of each bar's final (light-gray) segment, e.g. near (0.63, 0.27), (0.64, 0.42), (0.60, 0.55), (0.85, 0.69)
- Reference: no data labels anywhere on the chart.
- Candidate: a one-or-two-digit numeric label appears past the end of every bar, partially clipped by the chart frame's right edge.
- XML:
```xml
<c:dLbls>
  <c:showLegendKey val="0"/><c:showVal val="0"/><c:showCatName val="0"/>
  <c:showSerName val="0"/><c:showPercent val="0"/><c:showBubbleSize val="0"/>
</c:dLbls>
```
- Why the candidate is wrong: every `show*` flag on the chart-level `c:dLbls` is `0`, so no data label of any kind should be rendered on any series or point.

### 2. Value axis rendered on the left instead of the bottom, truncating category labels

- Where: Chart 10 (id 11), left label column, roughly x∈[0.06, 0.16], y∈[0.14, 0.78]
- Reference: category names ("Category 1"…"Category 4") shown in full on the left; value-axis ticks (0, 2, 4, …, 14) shown once, horizontally, along the bottom.
- Candidate: value-axis tick values (0, 3.1, 6.2, 9.2, 12.3) are interleaved vertically with the category labels in the same left column, and no value ticks appear along the bottom at all; the category text is squeezed to "Categ" because the column no longer has room for it.
- XML:
```xml
<c:catAx><c:axId val="579760832"/>...<c:axPos val="l"/>...<c:crossAx val="579764440"/></c:catAx>
<c:valAx><c:axId val="579764440"/>...<c:axPos val="b"/>...<c:crossAx val="579760832"/></c:valAx>
```
- Why the candidate is wrong: `c:valAx` has `c:axPos val="b"` — it must be laid out along the bottom edge of the plot area, not stacked into the category axis's left-hand label column.

### 3. Legend placed top-right instead of bottom-center

- Where: Chart 10 (id 11), region roughly x∈[0.86, 0.92], y∈[0.18, 0.24] in the candidate; reference legend sits at roughly x∈[0.40, 0.61], y∈[0.78, 0.81]
- Reference: a single horizontal row "■ Series 1  ■ Series 2  ■ Series 3" centered below the plot area.
- Candidate: the three legend entries are stacked vertically in the top-right corner of the chart frame.
- XML: `<c:legend><c:legendPos val="b"/>...</c:legend>`
- Why the candidate is wrong: `c:legendPos` is explicitly `"b"` (bottom); the candidate's placement matches neither a bottom nor even a right layout with correct margins.

### 4. Value axis auto-scale doesn't round to a "nice" max/step

- Where: Chart 10 (id 11), left label column, tick values 0/3.1/6.2/9.2/12.3
- Reference: axis runs 0 to 14 with a major unit of 2 (0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14) — a conventional rounded range above the true data max (12.3, from Category 4's 4.5+2.8+5 stack).
- Candidate: axis runs 0 to 12.3 with a major unit of ≈3.075, i.e. exactly the data max split into 4 equal ticks, with no rounding.
- XML: neither `c:valAx/c:scaling` nor the axis itself specifies `c:max`, `c:min`, or `c:majorUnit`, so both are auto-computed; the underlying data max across categories is 12.3 (Category 4: 4.5 + 2.8 + 5).
- Why the candidate is wrong: OOXML doesn't mandate a specific auto-scale algorithm, but not rounding the axis bound/step to a human-friendly number is a plausible renderer bug rather than a deliberate choice, and it changes the on-screen bar-length ratios between the two renders.
